# Clinical Progression Ladders for Seventeen Aphasia and Cognitive Linguistic Therapy Games

## Research frame

This synthesis follows the design brief you uploaded: one primary
progression axis per game, at most one secondary modifier, cueing
treated as orthogonal support rather than disguised content difficulty,
and honest confidence labels whenever the literature does not directly
validate a digital eight-tier ladder. fileciteturn0file0

A few global rules are well-supported. First, aphasia treatment should
be individualized and dynamically adjusted rather than treated as a
fixed protocol, which supports adaptive ladders in principle. ASHA's
Aphasia Practice Portal explicitly frames assessment and treatment as
individualized and dynamic, and current stroke guidance recommends
ongoing therapy while meaningful gains continue.
[\[1\]](https://www.asha.org/Practice-Portal/Clinical-Topics/Aphasia/)
Second, exact unlock thresholds for app progression are *not* directly
validated in the literature. The bands below are therefore calibration
defaults, not "proved" cutoffs: for closed-response tasks, roughly
75%--85% independent accuracy; for generative word-level tasks, roughly
70%--80% at lower levels and 75%--85% at upper levels; for
discourse/open-ended tasks, roughly 70%--80% criterion attainment on the
targeted rubric dimensions, rising modestly at higher tiers. That
approach is consistent with the Challenge Point Framework's emphasis on
keeping task difficulty functionally informative rather than trivially
easy or overwhelmingly hard, and with motor-learning tutorials used in
speech rehabilitation, but it remains a clinical implementation
heuristic rather than a published aphasia-specific constant.
[\[2\]](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Mark-Guadagnoli/publication/8574634_Challenge_Point_A_Framework_for_Conceptualizing_the_Effects_of_Various_Practice_Conditions_in_Motor_Learning/links/552d1b810cf2e089a3ad31e7/Challenge-Point-A-Framework-for-Conceptualizing-the-Effects-of-Various-Practice-Conditions-in-Motor-Learning.pdf)

A final methodological point mattered across almost every game. Lexical
access in aphasia is not a monolithic "word finding" problem: models of
speech production distinguish conceptual preparation, lexical selection,
and phonological form encoding; access-deficit work shows sensitivity to
cueing, competition, and inconsistency; and more recent treatment work
shows that semantic and phonological interventions can benefit different
anomic profiles. That means each ladder should target a single locus of
challenge instead of blending frequency, distractor load, time pressure,
and cue removal into one unstable scale.
[\[3\]](https://www.mpi.nl/world/materials/publications/Levelt/2001_PNAS.pdf)

## Semantic and lexical retrieval family

The cleanest family-level interpretation is that these games mostly
differ in *how* lexical-semantic activation is constrained or searched:
convergent retrieval, receptive discrimination, divergent search,
feature generation, or semantic foraging. That shared logic keeps the
family coherent for clinicians even when the operational axis differs by
game.
[\[4\]](https://www.mpi.nl/world/materials/publications/Levelt/2001_PNAS.pdf)

`two-clues`

**Clinical target.** This game targets convergent lexical retrieval from
sparse semantic information. Clinically, it fits anomia profiles where
semantic representations are at least partially available but efficient
access and selection are impaired; the task loads lexical-semantic
access and competition resolution more than articulation.
[\[5\]](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/369/1634/20120388/22342/What-we-talk-about-when-we-talk-about-access)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent the ability to identify a
target lexical item from increasingly weak or less diagnostic converging
semantic cues.

**Primary axis.** *Cue diagnosticity and convergence strength.* I infer
this is the cleanest axis because aphasic lexical retrieval is sensitive
to cue type and competitor load, and difficulty rises when semantic
evidence is less diagnostic or when close neighbors remain viable.
[\[6\]](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2021.747391/full)

**Secondary modifier.** Lexical frequency, used only within a tier band
when needed.

**Invalid axes.** Cue fade alone, because support reduction is not
content progression; time pressure alone, because it would mostly
measure speed/attention; phonological complexity, because the task is
not primarily phonological retrieval.

**Archetype.** Content-expanding.

**Cueing/support.** Keep support orthogonal: optional repetition,
semantic rephrase, then phonemic cue only after the independent attempt.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Two highly diagnostic clues for high-frequency concrete nouns;
example: "barks, leash" → *dog*; advance on draft band 70%--80%
independent.\
L2 --- Same format, but one clue becomes functional or associative
rather than near-definitional; "yellow, peel" → *banana*.\
L3 --- Concrete targets remain, but clue pairs now leave one plausible
semantic competitor active; "wings, sings" → *bird*.\
L4 --- Mid-frequency concrete nouns with two still-convergent clues;
"mail, stamp" → *letter*.\
L5 --- Lower-imageability or event nouns with relational clues; "vote,
office" → *election*.\
L6 --- Verb or role targets from thematic clues; "judge, courtroom" →
*lawyer* or "stitch, cloth" → *sew*.\
L7 --- Clues require suppression of a close foil or integration across
taxonomic and thematic knowledge; "hospital, infant" → *nursery* rather
than *doctor*.\
L8 --- Minimal-gist clue pairs for abstract or less imageable targets;
"fairness, law" → *justice*; advance on draft band 75%--85% independent.

**Evidence basis.** Lexical access models support cue-based narrowing
from semantic input to lexical selection; cueing studies show that cue
effectiveness depends on anomic profile; access-deficit work highlights
competitor strength and cue sensitivity as load-bearing variables.
[\[7\]](https://www.mpi.nl/world/materials/publications/Levelt/2001_PNAS.pdf)

**Confidence rating.** Exploratory. The construct is clinically
plausible, but I did not find a published aphasia protocol that directly
validates an eight-level *two-clue diagnosticity* ladder.

**Known integrity caveats.** If clues become easier solely because they
are longer, the ladder drifts from diagnosticity to verbosity; if
multiple acceptable targets exist, scoring must accept semantically
defensible alternatives; if phonemic hints are embedded in the clues,
the task stops being purely convergent semantic retrieval.

**Cross-game family.** Semantic family; closest to `meaning-match`,
`semantic-feature`, and `detective-mind`.

`semantic-feature`

**Clinical target.** This is the most direct implementation of Semantic
Feature Analysis for word retrieval. SFA has a meaningful aphasia
evidence base, especially for naming, and is most directly tied to
strengthening semantic activation around the target concept.
[\[8\]](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/68f09949-63e1-4827-af5b-f808453c410c)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent the ability to activate
and produce discriminative semantic features for increasingly less
imageable targets.

**Primary axis.** *Abstractness and imageability of the target concept.*
That axis is cleaner than "number of features" because the treatment
literature already links semantic complexity and abstractness to
generalization, whereas changing feature count alters task demands
rather than target complexity.
[\[9\]](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/treatment-for-lexical-retrieval-using-abstract-and-concrete-words)

**Secondary modifier.** None, if possible; keep required feature count
constant across tiers.

**Invalid axes.** Number of feature prompts as the main ladder; cue
hierarchy as the ladder; time pressure as the ladder.

**Archetype.** Content-expanding.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal fade from structured SFA chart with
exemplars, to category prompts only, to independent feature generation.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- High-imageability manipulable nouns; produce three acceptable
features; "apple" → *red, fruit, eat*.\
L2 --- High-imageability living things/household objects with slightly
lower typicality; "camel" → *desert, humps, animal*.\
L3 --- Mid-frequency concrete nouns with fewer salient perceptual
features; "ladder" → *climb, rungs, tall*.\
L4 --- Concrete verbs or role nouns requiring more relational than
perceptual features; "teacher" → *school, teaches, students*.\
L5 --- Less imageable concrete or institutional nouns; "receipt" →
*store, paper, purchase*.\
L6 --- Familiar abstract nouns with contextual/relational features;
"freedom" → *rights, choice, independence*.\
L7 --- Polysemous or relational concepts requiring features that
distinguish the target from close neighbors; "justice" → *law, fairness,
court*.\
L8 --- Low-imageability abstract or social concepts requiring three
discriminative, non-redundant features without a chart; advance on draft
band 75%--85% acceptable independent feature sets.

**Evidence basis.** SFA is effective for naming in acquired aphasia;
semantic treatment often outperforms phonological treatment at the group
level, especially for people with milder/fluent profiles; and Kiran's
complexity work supports abstractness as a clinically meaningful
semantic difficulty dimension.
[\[10\]](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/68f09949-63e1-4827-af5b-f808453c410c)

**Confidence rating.** Moderate. There is a real treatment base for SFA,
but the specific eight-tier digital ladder is still a synthesis.

**Known integrity caveats.** If later tiers only demand more features,
you are measuring endurance or working memory rather than semantic
complexity; if abstract items are used without clear scoring anchors,
reliability will drop; if cue-adjusted and independent performance are
merged, level estimates will be inflated.

**Cross-game family.** Semantic family; shares complexity logic with
`describe-guess` and `abstract-compare`.

`synonym-generator`

**Clinical target.** The one-liner is directionally right, but the
sharper construct is *controlled lexical-semantic selection among
near-equivalent alternatives*. "True synonymy" is rare; clinically, the
task is really about producing a context-appropriate near-synonym or
paraphrastic substitute while managing semantic competition.
[\[11\]](https://www.mpi.nl/world/materials/publications/Levelt/2001_PNAS.pdf)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent increasingly precise
selection within a crowded semantic neighborhood.

**Primary axis.** *Semantic competition and sense specificity.* I would
not use raw word frequency as the main axis here; the decisive
difficulty is usually whether the target's intended sense has many
near-neighbors or competing paraphrases.
[\[12\]](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/170094/3/Dyson%20Morgan%20Herbert%20Sem%20Proc%20in%20Aphasia.pdf)

**Secondary modifier.** Lexical frequency, only to stabilize item sets
inside tiers.

**Invalid axes.** Frequency as the whole ladder; cue fade; distractor
count, unless the task visibly includes foils.

**Archetype.** Content-expanding.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal support can include sentence context,
first-letter cue after failure, or forced-choice recognition on probe
trials.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- High-frequency adjectives or verbs with a dominant near-synonym
in a sentence context; "big" → *large*.\
L2 --- Common verbs with two acceptable high-frequency alternatives;
"start" → *begin*.\
L3 --- Concrete descriptive words whose substitute must preserve meaning
but not exact form; "tiny" → *small*.\
L4 --- Polysemous targets in disambiguating sentence frames; "bright" in
"a bright student" → *smart*.\
L5 --- Emotion or evaluative words with close competitors; "angry" →
*mad/upset*.\
L6 --- Lower-frequency abstract words requiring precise semantic
selection; "benefit" → *advantage*.\
L7 --- Context-sensitive terms where register or nuance matters; "help"
in formal text → *assist*.\
L8 --- Targets that may require short paraphrase rather than a
single-word substitute; scoring accepts semantically equivalent
paraphrases; advance on draft band 75%--85% context-appropriate
responses.

**Evidence basis.** Lexical access models and access-deficit work
support treating semantic competition as the central load; semantic
treatments improve naming more robustly than purely phonological
treatments at group level; and aphasic semantic difficulty is often most
visible when close semantic neighbors must be discriminated.
[\[13\]](https://www.mpi.nl/world/materials/publications/Levelt/2001_PNAS.pdf)

**Confidence rating.** Exploratory. The clinical logic is strong, but I
found little direct rehabilitation literature for synonym-generation
ladders specifically.

**Known integrity caveats.** If the game demands only one "correct"
answer, it will punish valid alternatives; if polysemy is uncontrolled,
the task will measure ambiguity resolution more than synonym generation;
if paraphrases are disallowed at high levels, the game may become
lexicographically artificial rather than clinically useful.

**Cross-game family.** Semantic family; nearest relatives are
`two-clues`, `meaning-match`, and `thought-continuation`.

`meaning-match`

**Clinical target.** This is best understood as receptive semantic
discrimination, not generic "vocabulary." Its clinical use is strongest
when the user must map a word, phrase, or sentence to its intended
meaning in the face of semantically related competitors. The adaptive
semantic matching literature is directly relevant here.
[\[14\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6045968/)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent increasingly fine-grained
receptive discrimination among close semantic competitors.

**Primary axis.** *Semantic distance between target and distractor.*
This is the cleanest, most literature-consistent axis because semantic
difficulty in aphasia often emerges when close neighbors must be
resolved, not necessarily when broad conceptual knowledge is absent.
[\[15\]](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/170094/3/Dyson%20Morgan%20Herbert%20Sem%20Proc%20in%20Aphasia.pdf)

**Secondary modifier.** Linguistic unit length, if needed, moving from
word-level to sentence-level stimuli.

**Invalid axes.** Visual complexity of pictures; time pressure as the
main ladder; cueing/support as the ladder.

**Archetype.** Content-expanding.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports can include stimulus repetition,
reduced foil count, or highlighting key words.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Word-to-picture or definition-to-word with unrelated foils;
*apple* vs car/chair/dog.\
L2 --- Same format with broad within-domain foils; *apple* vs
banana/orange/carrot.\
L3 --- Short definitions with closer semantic neighbors; "tool for
cutting paper" → *scissors* vs knife/razor.\
L4 --- Verb or role meanings with thematic competitors; "person who
teaches" → *teacher* vs student/principal.\
L5 --- Sentence-to-meaning matching with high lexical overlap across
foils.\
L6 --- Abstract word or sentence meanings with close paraphrases.\
L7 --- Short discourse or pragmatic gist matching with near-paraphrase
competitors.\
L8 --- High-similarity semantic judgments where all options are
plausible but only one best preserves intended meaning; advance on draft
band 80%--85% independent.

**Evidence basis.** Wilson's adaptive semantic matching paradigm shows
that semantic matching can be feasible, reliable, and valid in people
with aphasia; lexical-semantic comprehension treatment work confirms the
construct matters clinically; and semantic-interference work argues that
close-neighbor competition is a real load-bearing difficulty.
[\[14\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6045968/)

**Confidence rating.** Moderate. The assessment construct is well
grounded, even though the exact tiering is synthesized for digital use.

**Known integrity caveats.** If distractors vary wildly in word
frequency or visual salience, the ladder stops measuring semantic
distance cleanly; if sentence-level tiers add heavy syntax changes too
early, the game drifts into sentence comprehension rather than
semantics; if hints reduce foil similarity, support and content become
confounded.

**Cross-game family.** Semantic family; closest to `two-clues` and
`detective-mind`.

`describe-guess`

**Clinical target.** This mechanic is best characterized as expressive
circumlocution under communicative pressure, with strong overlap with
SFA-style feature activation and multimodal communication approaches
such as PACE and multimodal communication treatment. Clinically, it is
useful when direct naming fails but meaningful description can still
carry the message.
[\[16\]](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/68f09949-63e1-4827-af5b-f808453c410c)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent the ability to convey
increasingly less imageable targets through informative, discriminative
description without saying the target word.

**Primary axis.** *Target feature opacity, operationalized mainly by
abstractness and imageability.* Concrete items offer salient perceptual
and functional features; abstract targets force more relational and
contextual description.
[\[17\]](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/treatment-for-lexical-retrieval-using-abstract-and-concrete-words)

**Secondary modifier.** None preferred; keep the rule "describe until
guessed" constant.

**Invalid axes.** Number of allowed clues as the primary ladder;
guessing partner difficulty as the primary ladder; support fade as the
primary ladder.

**Archetype.** Open-ended.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports can include superordinate cue,
feature-wheel prompt, gesture permission, or communication-mode
reminders.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- High-imageability household objects; "You wear it on your feet in
bed" → *slippers*; band 70%--80% successful partner guesses.\
L2 --- High-imageability animals/tools with no color cue allowed.\
L3 --- Less typical concrete nouns requiring function and context rather
than appearance.\
L4 --- Role nouns and common actions requiring relational description.\
L5 --- Institutional or written-material items with fewer perceptual
features.\
L6 --- Familiar abstract nouns described through examples or
consequences.\
L7 --- Social-emotional concepts distinguished from close neighbors.\
L8 --- Low-imageability concepts where success depends on concise,
discriminative circumlocution rather than listing weak associates.

**Evidence basis.** SFA shows that activating semantic features helps
lexical retrieval; PACE and multimodal treatments support communicative
success via flexible message transmission rather than perfect naming;
and multimodal discourse studies show the clinical relevance of gesture,
writing, and other nonspeech supports.
[\[16\]](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/68f09949-63e1-4827-af5b-f808453c410c)

**Confidence rating.** Moderate. The communicative rationale is strong,
but formal dose-response evidence for this exact
"describe-until-guessed" ladder is limited.

**Known integrity caveats.** If the "guesser" is an AI with unstable
interpretation, tier scores may reflect model quirks rather than patient
skill; if target-word slips are automatically failed without considering
repair, the task punishes good compensatory behavior; if gesture/writing
are banned, the task stops resembling real-life circumlocution.

**Cross-game family.** Semantic family; closest to `semantic-feature`
and `conversation-coach`.

`category-fluency`

**Clinical target.** Semantic verbal fluency in aphasia reflects both
lexical retrieval and executive search processes, especially clustering
and switching between subcategories. The literature consistently warns
against treating it as a pure language score.
[\[18\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9314833/)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent increasingly efficient
semantic search and switching under a fixed time constraint.

**Primary axis.** *Semantic search-control demand*, operationalized by
how broad, heterogeneous, and switching-heavy the category is.

**Secondary modifier.** Time window may be modestly shortened only after
the category-set complexity is already stable.

**Invalid axes.** Word frequency alone; cue fade alone; articulation
speed alone.

**Archetype.** Hybrid.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports can include visible subcategory
anchors, starter exemplars, or metacognitive reminders to switch
clusters.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Tight, highly prototypical categories such as *farm animals* in
60 seconds.\
L2 --- Slightly broader concrete categories such as *fruits*.\
L3 --- Large but still concrete categories such as *animals*.\
L4 --- Broad thematic sets requiring subcategory switches, such as
*things in a kitchen*.\
L5 --- Categories with weaker internal structure, such as *things you
take on a trip*.\
L6 --- Categories that require controlled search and stopping rules,
such as *things used for repair*.\
L7 --- Abstract or socially organized categories, such as *ways people
communicate* or *things that show trust*.\
L8 --- Very broad categories where success depends on sustained
switching and self-monitoring, with the same one-minute constraint;
upper-tier band 75%--85% of individualized item-count or cluster-switch
target.

**Evidence basis.** Aphasia fluency work shows that reduced output
reflects both lexical and executive contributors, with switching and
clustering carrying clinically meaningful information; verbal fluency
studies in aphasia support using search-control burden rather than raw
category familiarity as the difficulty logic.
[\[19\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9314833/)

**Confidence rating.** Moderate. The task mechanics are common and the
underlying processes are well described, though formal therapy ladders
are less standardized.

**Known integrity caveats.** Scores should be individualized because
education and baseline semantic knowledge matter; if time pressure is
changed at the same time category breadth changes, the ladder becomes
hard to interpret; if repeated categories are reused too often, practice
effects will contaminate progression.

**Cross-game family.** Semantic family with an executive border; most
closely related to `semantic-feature` and `pattern-match`.

## Syntax, sentence, and discourse family

This family is strongest when it keeps the unit of progression visible
to the clinician. For sentence-level games, that unit is usually
syntactic complexity. For discourse-level games, it is usually
macrostructure, coherence, inferential burden, or degree of contextual
under-specification. The weakest designs in this family are the ones
that quietly mix memory length, syntax, and cue withdrawal into a single
"harder" label.
[\[20\]](https://asha.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Treatment_of_underlying_forms_A_meta-analysis_Swiderski_et_al_2021_/16828630)

`sentence-construction`

**Clinical target.** This is a classic expressive syntax task. The
strongest treatment analogues are Treatment of Underlying Forms and
mapping-based sentence therapy, both of which treat sentence-level
deficits by targeting increasingly complex sentence structures rather
than arbitrary word-list difficulty.
[\[21\]](https://asha.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Treatment_of_underlying_forms_A_meta-analysis_Swiderski_et_al_2021_/16828630)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent the ability to produce
increasingly complex sentence structures accurately.

**Primary axis.** *Syntactic complexity.*

**Secondary modifier.** None preferred; keep lexical support as constant
as possible.

**Invalid axes.** Word frequency of sentence content; cue fade; sentence
length by itself.

**Archetype.** Content-expanding.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports can include word-order tiles,
thematic-role highlighting, model sentences, or forced-choice previews.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Canonical, nonreversible active sentences with simple transitive
or intransitive frames.\
L2 --- Canonical actives with modifiers or prepositional phrases.\
L3 --- Tense/agreement marking added while sentence structure remains
canonical.\
L4 --- Reversible active sentences where thematic roles must be encoded
correctly.\
L5 --- Noncanonical but common structures such as passives or
object-focused constructions.\
L6 --- Relative clauses or complement clauses with one embedding.\
L7 --- Multi-clause sentences with reversible thematic roles or more
than one syntactic dependency.\
L8 --- Highest-complexity noncanonical sentences or embedded structures
produced from content words/pictures without overt word-order cueing;
band 75%--85% structurally correct independent productions.

**Evidence basis.** The TUF meta-analysis found group-level evidence for
efficacy and generalization consistent with the Complexity Account of
Treatment Efficacy, including gains from more complex to less complex
related structures; mapping therapy likewise treats sentence production
by role-to-structure mapping.
[\[21\]](https://asha.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Treatment_of_underlying_forms_A_meta-analysis_Swiderski_et_al_2021_/16828630)

**Confidence rating.** High. This is the best-grounded ladder family in
the entire set.

**Known integrity caveats.** If later tiers increase only sentence
length but not structure, the ladder ceases to be syntactic; if
correctness is judged only by keywords rather than structure, severe
agrammatism can be masked; if semantic reversibility is uncontrolled,
some items become easier for conceptual rather than syntactic reasons.

**Cross-game family.** Syntax/discourse family; closest to
`narrative-retell` and `thought-continuation`.

`narrative-retell`

**Clinical target.** Retell tasks exercise comprehension-to-production
transfer at discourse level. The clinically defensible targets are main
concepts, sequencing, story grammar, and propositional/macrostructural
organization---not just "story length." AphasiaBank and recent
discourse-treatment work support those as legitimate discourse metrics.
[\[22\]](https://talkbank.org/aphasia/discourse/StoryGrammar/index.html)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent accurate recovery and
production of increasingly complex discourse macrostructure.

**Primary axis.** *Story-grammar and macrostructural complexity*,
operationalized by number of episodes, main concepts, and episodic
complexity.

**Secondary modifier.** Delay interval may be added cautiously at upper
tiers.

**Invalid axes.** Mere word count; cue fade alone; audio length alone
without macrostructural profiling.

**Archetype.** Content-expanding.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports include pictures, retell
outline, character map, or first-event prompt.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Single-episode stories with explicit setting, event, and
outcome.\
L2 --- Single-episode stories with one internal response or goal state
added.\
L3 --- Single-episode stories with more propositions but the same
structure.\
L4 --- Two-episode stories with a straightforward chronology.\
L5 --- Two-episode stories with distractor details and denser
proposition load.\
L6 --- Multi-character stories requiring maintenance of referents and
causal links.\
L7 --- Stories with embedded goals, shifts in perspective, or higher
inferential demand.\
L8 --- Untrained narratives retold after a brief delay, scored on main
concepts, sequencing, and essential story grammar rather than mere
length.

**Evidence basis.** Nicholas and Brookshire established main concepts as
a clinically useful discourse summary metric; story-grammar frameworks
identify setting, initiating event, response/goal, plan, attempt, and
consequence as core macrostructural elements; MSSG work and LUNA both
support multilevel discourse treatment design.
[\[23\]](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/7731205)

**Confidence rating.** Moderate. The discourse metrics are defensible,
but retell-specific ladder validation remains thinner than
sentence-production evidence.

**Known integrity caveats.** If upper tiers reuse trained stories, they
do not assess generalization; if scoring ignores sequencing and main
concepts, verbosity can masquerade as success; if delay is added too
early, the ladder becomes a memory test rather than a discourse test.

**Cross-game family.** Discourse family; closest to
`thought-continuation`, `conversation-coach`, and `detective-mind`.

`thought-continuation`

**Clinical target.** The sharper construct here is *self-initiated
propositional generation with local discourse coherence*, not "lexical
prediction." In aphasia, discourse coherence depends heavily on language
processes and topic maintenance, with working memory also contributing,
especially when the context is underspecified.
[\[24\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9240415/)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent the ability to generate
contextually coherent continuation as external constraint decreases.

**Primary axis.** *Contextual under-specification*, operationalized by
how much of the upcoming proposition must be generated rather than
recovered from a strongly constraining stem.

**Secondary modifier.** Abstractness of topic.

**Invalid axes.** Sentence length by itself; support fade; grammar-only
scoring.

**Archetype.** Open-ended.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports include semantic starter words,
discourse organizer prompts, or partner confirmation checks.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Highly constraining sentence stems with one obvious continuation:
"She put on her coat because ...".\
L2 --- Short stems with several acceptable continuations but the same
local topic.\
L3 --- Two-sentence contexts requiring one causal or temporal
proposition.\
L4 --- Contexts requiring maintenance of pronominal reference and
topical coherence.\
L5 --- Less constrained social or daily-life prompts requiring two
linked propositions.\
L6 --- Emotionally or conceptually abstract prompts requiring coherent
elaboration.\
L7 --- Prompts with multiple plausible discourse directions, where the
speaker must choose and sustain one.\
L8 --- Open thought continuation over several turns/utterances with no
supplied lexical scaffold; upper-tier band should weight coherence,
informativeness, and independence more than sentence count.

**Evidence basis.** Discourse coherence in aphasia is a real and
measurable construct; discourse therapy research increasingly targets
suprasentential production; and working memory contributes to
integration of information over discourse.
[\[25\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9240415/)

**Confidence rating.** Exploratory. The construct is clinically real,
but direct therapy-protocol evidence specific to "thought continuation"
is limited.

**Known integrity caveats.** If raters privilege grammar over coherence,
the task becomes a sentence-level game in disguise; if semantic prompts
are too strong, the ladder collapses into constrained sentence
completion; if topic novelty and abstractness change simultaneously,
interpretation becomes messy.

**Cross-game family.** Discourse family; closest to `narrative-retell`
and `conversation-partner`.

`abstract-compare`

**Clinical target.** The one-liner is close, but the sharper construct
is *verbal relational reasoning across concept pairs*. This combines
semantic judgment with discourse organization. Abstractness is
clinically meaningful because people with aphasia often show a
concreteness advantage, and treatment studies have used abstractness as
a legitimate complexity dimension.
[\[26\]](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/treatment-for-lexical-retrieval-using-abstract-and-concrete-words)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent increasingly successful
verbal comparison of concepts as conceptual abstractness rises.

**Primary axis.** *Abstractness and imageability of the concept pair.*

**Secondary modifier.** Degree of relational overlap between the pair.

**Invalid axes.** Sentence length alone; cue fade; topic familiarity
alone.

**Archetype.** Open-ended.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports include comparison frame ("same
because... different because..."), graphic organizer, or example then
fade.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Compare concrete objects with obvious perceptual overlap: *apple
vs orange*.\
L2 --- Compare concrete objects with functional rather than visual
overlap: *fork vs spoon*.\
L3 --- Compare activities or roles requiring relational language:
*teacher vs coach*.\
L4 --- Compare familiar social concepts: *friendship vs teamwork*.\
L5 --- Compare common emotional or evaluative concepts: *happy vs
proud*.\
L6 --- Compare institutional or social abstractions: *freedom vs
privacy*.\
L7 --- Compare closely related abstract values where distinctions are
subtle: *justice vs fairness*.\
L8 --- Compare low-imageability abstract pairs with both similarities
and differences justified in context; advancing should rely on a rubric
for coherence, relevance, and conceptual precision.

**Evidence basis.** Abstractness has treated utility in aphasia naming
work; semantic judgment ability is measurably affected in aphasia; and
discourse/pragmatics theory places abstract concept comparison within
higher-level meaning making.
[\[27\]](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/treatment-for-lexical-retrieval-using-abstract-and-concrete-words)

**Confidence rating.** Exploratory. The abstractness dimension is
defensible, but I did not find a direct protocol for graded
concept-comparison therapy in aphasia.

**Known integrity caveats.** If factual knowledge dominates, the task
can become an education test; if concept pairs are unevenly culturally
loaded, scoring fairness suffers; if response quality is scored only by
length, circumlocutory but shallow answers may be overrated.

**Cross-game family.** Semantic/discourse border family; closest to
`semantic-feature` and `thought-continuation`.

## Executive and cognitive linguistic family

These games live partly inside aphasia rehabilitation and partly inside
broader cognitive rehabilitation. The literature supports them best when
they are framed as *cognitive-communication* tasks rather than generic
brain games, and when goals remain functional, metacognitive, and
context-relevant. INCOG 2.0 is particularly helpful here.
[\[28\]](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/8ba6ea6c-1191-ed11-8141-005056834e2b)

`detective-mind`

**Clinical target.** This game is essentially inferential reasoning from
language input. If the clues are textual, it also loads reading or
listening comprehension; if they are purely verbal, it loads
discourse-level integration, working memory, and bridging inference.
Aphasia discourse literature and inference-processing studies support
this sharpening. [\[29\]](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ730876)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent successful integration of
increasingly implicit clue sets into a single inferred conclusion.

**Primary axis.** *Inferential load.*

**Secondary modifier.** Working memory load through number of clues held
simultaneously.

**Invalid axes.** Word frequency alone; time pressure alone; support
fade alone.

**Archetype.** Hybrid.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports include clue reread/replay,
note-taking scaffold, or explicit elimination prompts.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- One explicit clue leads directly to the answer.\
L2 --- Two explicit clues must be integrated.\
L3 --- One clue becomes indirect, requiring a simple bridging
inference.\
L4 --- Short paragraphs require cause--effect integration.\
L5 --- Paragraphs include distractor details, so the target depends on
relevance filtering.\
L6 --- Multi-sentence clues require pronoun/reference tracking plus
inference.\
L7 --- Several plausible candidates remain until late evidence resolves
them.\
L8 --- Inferential reasoning includes implied social or emotional
states, not only explicit facts; upper-tier band should combine accuracy
with low support dependence.

**Evidence basis.** Aphasia discourse/pragmatics work highlights
implicatures and implied information as clinically relevant; inference
revision studies show not all aphasia profiles handle inferences
equally; and working-memory literature supports a resource contribution
to discourse integration.
[\[30\]](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-031-77134-7_6)

**Confidence rating.** Exploratory to moderate. The components are
documented, but the exact game mechanic is a synthesis.

**Known integrity caveats.** If later tiers add unusual vocabulary
instead of more inference, the ladder drifts into lexical testing; if
clue count rises too aggressively, the game becomes mostly a memory span
task; if reading is required, accessibility features matter for
non-reading aphasia.

**Cross-game family.** Executive/discourse border family; closest to
`meaning-match` and `narrative-retell`.

`multi-step-plan`

**Clinical target.** This is a cognitive-communication planning task
aimed at sequencing, organization, and goal management. The closest
evidence base is executive rehabilitation rather than aphasia-specific
naming therapy, especially metacognitive strategy training and
goal-management approaches for planning and problem solving.
[\[31\]](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/8ba6ea6c-1191-ed11-8141-005056834e2b)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent the ability to build and
maintain increasingly deep goal-directed action sequences.

**Primary axis.** *Plan depth and working-memory load*, operationalized
by number of ordered steps and subgoal nesting.

**Secondary modifier.** Constraint density, introduced sparingly at
upper tiers.

**Invalid axes.** Raw linguistic complexity of step wording; cue fade
alone; speed alone.

**Archetype.** Content-expanding.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports include visible step cards,
plan-do-check prompts, and error-review feedback.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Order three familiar steps for a daily routine.\
L2 --- Order four to five familiar steps with one clear endpoint.\
L3 --- Plan five steps with one supporting subgoal.\
L4 --- Plan six steps where one step must precede another for
tool/resource reasons.\
L5 --- Six to seven steps with a simple constraint such as "before
leaving home."\
L6 --- Multi-location or multi-resource plans with more than one
dependency.\
L7 --- Plans requiring monitoring or revision after a changed
condition.\
L8 --- Hierarchical plans with several subgoals and constraint
management; advance on a rubric for correct order, constraint
satisfaction, and self-monitoring, not just final sequence accuracy.

**Evidence basis.** INCOG recommends metacognitive strategy instruction
for planning, organization, and problem solving, including
self-monitoring and feedback; goal management training was designed
specifically for planning and goal-directed behavior deficits; and
post-stroke executive reviews confirm the functional relevance of these
impairments.
[\[31\]](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/8ba6ea6c-1191-ed11-8141-005056834e2b)

**Confidence rating.** Moderate. The executive rehabilitation basis is
good, but aphasia-specific digital tier validation is limited.

**Known integrity caveats.** If upper tiers add linguistic complexity in
the step wording, the game can become a sentence-comprehension task; if
dependency rules are implicit rather than visible in item design, errors
become hard to interpret; if scoring ignores self-correction, clinically
useful metacognitive gains are missed.

**Cross-game family.** Executive family; closest to `pattern-match` and
`detective-mind`.

`pattern-match`

**Clinical target.** This game is less aphasia-specific than several
others. Unless the patterns are verbalized or semantically mediated, it
is better framed as a nonlinguistic or mixed rule-induction task inside
a cognitive-communication program, not as core aphasia therapy. That
does not make it useless; it just changes the claim.
[\[32\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24559523/)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent increasingly successful
induction and application of abstract rules.

**Primary axis.** *Rule induction complexity.*

**Secondary modifier.** Working memory load through sequence length.

**Invalid axes.** Visual busyness; time pressure as the main ladder;
support fade as the main ladder.

**Archetype.** Content-expanding.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports include explicit "find the rule"
framing, highlighted comparisons, or self-explanation prompts.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Single-feature repetition or alternation.\
L2 --- Simple one-rule progressions.\
L3 --- Two-state alternations that require tracking order.\
L4 --- One rule with a missing element or gap-fill.\
L5 --- Second-order or nested rules.\
L6 --- Mixed semantic/visual patterns with the same underlying rule
type.\
L7 --- Patterns with misleading local regularities that must be
inhibited.\
L8 --- Transfer of a learned rule family to new exemplars with no overt
hint.

**Evidence basis.** Rule-induction work indicates that performance
changes with complexity and executive load; broader executive-function
frameworks also support distinguishing simple from higher-order rule
discovery. But direct aphasia-treatment evidence for this exact mechanic
is sparse. [\[32\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24559523/)

**Confidence rating.** Exploratory.

**Known integrity caveats.** If the task is purely visual, it should not
be oversold as language rehabilitation; if semantic-labeling demands are
added inconsistently, the ladder mixes nonverbal reasoning and language;
if the user cannot externalize the rule, observed failures may reflect
expression rather than induction.

**Cross-game family.** Executive family; closest to `multi-step-plan`
and `category-fluency`.

`dual-load-naming`

**Clinical target.** This game targets naming under divided attention.
Recent dual-task studies in aphasia show slower naming latencies and, at
certain overlaps, increased phonological errors when a concurrent
auditory task is imposed. That makes this task clinically sharper than
generic "harder naming": it probes attentional sharing during lexical
and phonological encoding.
[\[33\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0028393225000715)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent preserved naming under
increasing concurrent-task interference.

**Primary axis.** *Dual-task interference magnitude*, operationalized by
the overlap, modality, and control burden of the secondary task while
naming stimuli stay roughly stable.

**Secondary modifier.** Lexical frequency of targets, only if needed to
stabilize upper tiers.

**Invalid axes.** Cue fade; distractor count in the naming set; changing
both naming difficulty and load difficulty at the same time.

**Archetype.** Hybrid.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports include slower pace, simplified
instructions, or load preview practice.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Picture naming with very light intermittent nonverbal
monitoring.\
L2 --- Same naming set with more frequent nonverbal monitoring.\
L3 --- Concurrent low-rate auditory detection with closer temporal
overlap to naming.\
L4 --- Simple counting or categorization load runs alongside naming.\
L5 --- Continuous auditory load that requires updating while naming
remains otherwise stable.\
L6 --- Verbal concurrent load or one-back style monitoring with
controlled overlap.\
L7 --- Less predictable dual-task timing requiring flexible allocation.\
L8 --- Dual-task naming under the highest stable overlap tolerated,
scored on accuracy and phonological error rate rather than latency
alone.

**Evidence basis.** Dual-task studies show slower naming and more
phonological errors in people with aphasia when concurrent auditory
tasks are introduced; attention models in aphasia also support shared
central resources during language production.
[\[33\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0028393225000715)

**Confidence rating.** Moderate. The interference construct is directly
supported, though a digital ladder still requires calibration.

**Known integrity caveats.** If naming targets also get less frequent
across tiers, the ladder becomes uninterpretable; if latency is the only
outcome, accurate-but-slow compensatory performance may be mislabeled
failure; if the secondary task is verbal, part of the effect may reflect
phonological overlap rather than generic divided attention.

**Cross-game family.** Executive/language interface family; closest to
`phonological` and `photo-naming` style naming tasks.

## Phonological, acoustic, and conversational family

This section combines the most sublexical task in the set with the most
interactional tasks. They do not share content, but they do share a
strong need for clean construct definition. In phonology, lower-level
acoustic and phonemic processing can be real bottlenecks in aphasia. In
conversation, the evidence is clearest when therapy targets
interactional behaviors, partner training, and functional participation
rather than abstract "better talking."
[\[34\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-37624-w.pdf)

`phonological`

**Clinical target.** The one-liner should be sharpened to *sublexical
phonological analysis and manipulation*. Post-stroke aphasia can involve
impaired acoustic and phonemic processing, and phonological treatments
such as the Phonomotor approach and related phonological interventions
explicitly target strengthening phoneme-level representations and
operations.
[\[35\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-37624-w.pdf)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent increasingly complex
phonological manipulation performed accurately and with minimal lexical
support.

**Primary axis.** *Segmental manipulation complexity*, operationalized
by operation type, segment position, and phonological markedness/cluster
complexity.

**Secondary modifier.** Lexicality, moving carefully toward nonwords in
upper tiers.

**Invalid axes.** Orthographic difficulty; cue fade; broad vocabulary
frequency.

**Archetype.** Content-expanding.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports include repetition, visual
phoneme cards, articulatory placement cues, or slowed audio.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Same/different rhyme or initial-sound judgments for simple real
words.\
L2 --- Identify onset or final phoneme in simple CVC words.\
L3 --- Blend and segment simple CVC words.\
L4 --- Delete onset or coda from simple real words.\
L5 --- Substitute one phoneme in singletons.\
L6 --- Manipulate words with consonant clusters or multisyllabic
structure.\
L7 --- Perform deletion/substitution on nonwords or very low-support
real-word strings.\
L8 --- Chain two phonological operations or manipulate marked structures
with minimal support; upper-tier band 75%--85% accurate independent
manipulations.

**Evidence basis.** Low-level acoustic and phonemic processing are
impaired in many people with aphasia and can predict higher-level
phonological performance; phonomotor and phonological treatment work
supports targeting phoneme-level representations; and
phonological-network work suggests structural phonological relations
matter for generalization.
[\[36\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-37624-w.pdf)

**Confidence rating.** Moderate. The phonological treatment rationale is
solid, although the exact task mix in your game will affect how cleanly
this ladder fits.

**Known integrity caveats.** If orthographic display is present, the
game may partially become a reading task; if real-word lexical support
is strong at all tiers, upper levels may under-challenge phonological
manipulation; if acoustic fidelity is poor, errors may reflect hearing
or device quality rather than phonological skill.

**Cross-game family.** Phonological family; closest to `minimal-pairs`
and `dual-load-naming`.

`conversation-coach`

**Clinical target.** This should be positioned as structured
conversation training using partner support and video/interaction
feedback, conceptually closest to Supported Conversation for Adults with
Aphasia and Better Conversations with Aphasia rather than generic "chat
practice." The literature supports partner training, barrier reduction,
and individualized social-communication goals.
[\[37\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003999316300740)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent success in increasingly
complex structured interaction while supports remain available
orthogonally.

**Primary axis.** *Interactional complexity*, operationalized by number
of turns, topic shifts, repair opportunities, and discourse moves
required within a structured exchange.

**Secondary modifier.** Topic novelty.

**Invalid axes.** Cue fade as the whole ladder; partner
warmth/personality variance; lexical frequency.

**Archetype.** Open-ended.

**Cueing/support.** Keep SCA-style cueing orthogonal: written key words,
verification checks, gesture prompts, choice questions, or video
feedback can fade separately from topic complexity.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Routine adjacency pairs on familiar topics with explicit goal and
heavy scaffold.\
L2 --- Short scripted exchanges with equal turn-taking and predictable
follow-up.\
L3 --- Three-turn maintenance on one topic with one new-information
exchange.\
L4 --- Structured repair within the exchange after one breakdown.\
L5 --- Brief narrative or opinion insertion inside a familiar topic.\
L6 --- Topic extension with question generation and clarification.\
L7 --- Multi-topic conversation plan with transitions and reduced
predictability.\
L8 --- Near-natural but still coached interaction featuring topic
initiation, maintenance, repair, and closing; scoring should weight
barrier reduction and successful exchange, not only word count.

**Evidence basis.** Communication partner training has systematic-review
support; Better Conversations found reductions in conversation barriers
and change in targeted conversational behaviors after an eight-session
program; national stroke guidance and INCOG both recommend communication
partner training and social-communication interventions.
[\[38\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003999316300740)

**Confidence rating.** Moderate. The conversation-treatment basis is
real, although digital operationalization remains partly translational.

**Known integrity caveats.** If the system scores only linguistic output
and ignores repair/barrier behavior, it misses the clinical point; if
partner prompts vary unpredictably, patient progression becomes noisy;
if scripted dialogue is too rigid at all tiers, the game measures
compliance rather than conversation.

**Cross-game family.** Conversational family; closest to
`conversation-partner`, `describe-guess`, and `narrative-retell`.

`conversation-partner`

**Clinical target.** This is best framed as functional, less scripted
conversation emphasizing self-initiation, turn-taking, repair, and
pragmatic contingency. Compared with `conversation-coach`, it is a more
participation-oriented measure of everyday discourse.
[\[39\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9567347/)

**Core construct.** Progress should represent increasingly independent
management of pragmatic demands in open conversation.

**Primary axis.** *Pragmatic contingency burden*, operationalized by how
much the speaker must self-initiate, maintain topic, manage repair, and
respond to less predictable partner behavior.

**Secondary modifier.** Social-cognitive demand of the topic.

**Invalid axes.** Cue fade as the ladder; conversation length alone;
lexical sophistication alone.

**Archetype.** Open-ended.

**Cueing/support.** Orthogonal supports include repair options, topic
board, partner model pacing, or recap prompts.

**Candidate L1--L8 ladder.**\
L1 --- Familiar-topic exchange with explicit conversation goal.\
L2 --- Open partner questions on a familiar topic.\
L3 --- Self-initiate and maintain one topic for several turns.\
L4 --- Manage one clarification request or repair sequence.\
L5 --- Integrate reference to shared background or prior turn content.\
L6 --- Shift topics appropriately and return after breakdown.\
L7 --- Handle disagreement, stance, or emotionally nuanced discussion.\
L8 --- Fully open conversation with initiation, maintenance, repair, and
closure managed primarily by the person with aphasia; band should be
rubric-based.

**Evidence basis.** Conversation is a high-priority outcome in aphasia
treatment; self-repair is interactionally meaningful in aphasia; partner
training and social-communication guidelines emphasize personally
relevant contexts and functional outcomes rather than narrow impairment
metrics.
[\[40\]](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/epdf/10.1044/2022_AJSLP-22-00011)

**Confidence rating.** Exploratory to moderate. The participation target
is clinically important, but standardized tier design is difficult and
the literature still reports measurement gaps.

**Known integrity caveats.** If partner behavior changes from session to
session, the ladder becomes partly a partner test; if the AI/listener is
overly accommodating, upper-tier demands may be artificially low; if
conversation quality is scored without considering participation goals,
the measure may not reflect real-world communicative success.

**Cross-game family.** Conversational/discourse family; closest to
`conversation-coach` and `thought-continuation`.

## Family synthesis

**Semantic and lexical family.** `two-clues`, `semantic-feature`,
`synonym-generator`, `meaning-match`, `describe-guess`, and
`category-fluency` can share a coherent taxonomy if you describe them as
different forms of lexical-semantic control. `meaning-match` is the
clearest *receptive discrimination* member, `semantic-feature` and
`describe-guess` are *feature-based activation* members, `two-clues` is
*convergent retrieval*, `synonym-generator` is *competitive divergent
selection*, and `category-fluency` is *semantic search and switching*.
The potential inconsistency to avoid is using raw lexical frequency as
the master axis for some games and semantic competition for others;
frequency is often a useful item-matching covariate, but not usually the
cleanest family-level primary axis.
[\[41\]](https://www.mpi.nl/world/materials/publications/Levelt/2001_PNAS.pdf)

**Syntax and discourse family.** `sentence-construction`,
`narrative-retell`, `thought-continuation`, and `abstract-compare` are
coherent if each is explicit about its unit of progression.
`sentence-construction` should stay syntactic. `narrative-retell` should
stay macrostructural. `thought-continuation` should stay about coherence
under under-specification. `abstract-compare` should stay about
relational-semantic abstraction. The main risk is letting all four drift
into generic "harder speaking."
[\[20\]](https://asha.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Treatment_of_underlying_forms_A_meta-analysis_Swiderski_et_al_2021_/16828630)

**Executive and cognitive-linguistic family.** `detective-mind`,
`multi-step-plan`, `pattern-match`, and `dual-load-naming` can be
explained under a cognitive-communication umbrella, but the evidence is
uneven. `multi-step-plan` and `dual-load-naming` have the clearest
rehabilitative logic. `detective-mind` is defensible if inferential
load, not vocabulary difficulty, drives the ladder. `pattern-match` is
the most likely to be misclassified as language therapy when it is
actually broader executive reasoning.
[\[42\]](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/8ba6ea6c-1191-ed11-8141-005056834e2b)

**Phonological and conversational family.** `phonological`,
`conversation-coach`, and `conversation-partner` do not share a content
axis, but each has a relatively clean clinical target. The phonological
game should not quietly become reading or orthography. The conversation
games should not quietly become "produce more words." Their value is in
sublexical robustness for the former, and functional interaction,
repair, and participation for the latter.
[\[43\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-37624-w.pdf)

**Cross-family axis conflicts to resolve or flag.** The biggest
conflicts are conceptual, not empirical. `category-fluency` sits between
semantic and executive families; label it explicitly as *semantic
fluency with executive search demands*. `pattern-match` sits outside
classic aphasia treatment; if kept, its about-page should call it
*cognitive-communication* rather than language restoration.
`thought-continuation` is better explained as discourse coherence than
lexical prediction. `abstract-compare` is closer to abstract semantic
judgment plus discourse organization than to simple naming. Finally,
exact eight-tier cutoffs remain draft clinical calibration defaults
across all families, not published validation thresholds.
[\[44\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9314833/)

## Consolidated references

The most load-bearing sources for the ladder designs were the ones
below.

Boyle, M., and related SFA review literature; ASHA's evidence summary
concludes that semantic feature analysis is effective for confrontation
naming in adults with acquired aphasia, with larger effects often
reported in fluent aphasia.
[\[45\]](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/68f09949-63e1-4827-af5b-f808453c410c)

Kiran, S., Sandberg, C., and Abbott, K. showed that treating abstract
words as the more complex semantic level can support generalization to
untrained concrete words in aphasia, which is why abstractness is usable
as a progression axis in several semantic games.
[\[46\]](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/treatment-for-lexical-retrieval-using-abstract-and-concrete-words)

Levelt's lexical-access theory and Dell's interactive lexical-retrieval
model remain the clearest foundations for separating conceptual
activation, lexical selection, and form encoding in naming-related
games.
[\[47\]](https://www.mpi.nl/world/materials/publications/Levelt/2001_PNAS.pdf)

Mirman and Britt's access-deficit synthesis, together with newer cueing
studies, supports competitor-sensitive lexical retrieval and explains
why cue diagnosticity and semantic competition are cleaner axes than
generic difficulty labels.
[\[48\]](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/369/1634/20120388/22342/What-we-talk-about-when-we-talk-about-access)

Wilson and colleagues' adaptive semantic matching paradigm supports
`meaning-match`-style receptive semantics work because it was feasible,
reliable, and valid in people with aphasia.
[\[49\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6045968/)

Dyson and colleagues' semantic-processing work is important because it
emphasizes that aphasic semantic difficulty often appears when close
semantic neighbors must be resolved, supporting distractor similarity as
a primary axis.
[\[50\]](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/170094/3/Dyson%20Morgan%20Herbert%20Sem%20Proc%20in%20Aphasia.pdf)

Swiderski and colleagues' meta-analysis of Treatment of Underlying Forms
gives the strongest sentence-level ladder foundation in the set,
including evidence consistent with complexity-driven generalization.
[\[51\]](https://asha.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Treatment_of_underlying_forms_A_meta-analysis_Swiderski_et_al_2021_/16828630)

Nicholas and Brookshire's main-concept work, AphasiaBank story-grammar
resources, Richardson and colleagues' MSSG paper, and the LUNA discourse
framework jointly support macrostructural progression for narrative
tasks. [\[52\]](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/7731205)

Bose, Wood, Kiran, and later verbal-fluency studies show that fluency
performance in aphasia depends on both lexical retrieval and executive
control, especially clustering and switching, which is why
`category-fluency` should not be treated as a pure vocabulary task.
[\[53\]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/1460-6984.12276)

INCOG 2.0 executive and cognitive-communication guidelines, together
with stroke communication guidance, provide the best currently available
scaffold for the executive and conversation games, especially around
planning, self-monitoring, communication partner training, group/social
communication, and functionally relevant contexts.
[\[54\]](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/8ba6ea6c-1191-ed11-8141-005056834e2b)

Kagan's Supported Conversation for Adults with Aphasia trial, the
communication-partner training systematic review, and Better
Conversations with Aphasia provide the strongest base for
conversation-focused games.
[\[55\]](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11407567)

Recent dual-task naming studies and work on impaired acoustic/phonemic
processing in aphasia are the critical sources for `dual-load-naming`
and `phonological`, respectively.
[\[56\]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0028393225000715)
