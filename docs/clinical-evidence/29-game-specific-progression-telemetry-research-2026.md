# NeuroSpark — game-specific clinical constructs, progression and telemetry
**Research synthesis, 2026-11-02 · PROPOSED; CLINICAL VALIDITY NOT ESTABLISHED · 29 adult activities**

This is a research-to-engineering specification for clinician discussion and software auditing. It does **not** prescribe independent medical care, prove eight-tier validity, certify efficacy, or authorize changing persisted clinical progression thresholds. Review with a speech-language pathologist (SLP), neuropsychologist and OT/PT as appropriate before claiming a metric as a clinical outcome.

## What the research establishes

1. Aphasia rehabilitation must be personalized to the person's language profile, goals, communication partners and participation priorities. The European Stroke Organisation 2025 aphasia guideline supports digital speech and language therapy as an adjunct or alternative delivery mode and recommends tailoring to functionally relevant difficulty, but **does not validate NeuroSpark's eight levels or universal percentage cutoffs**. [ESO 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12098336/) and [Canadian Stroke Best Practices 2025](https://www.strokebestpractices.ca/recommendations/stroke-rehabilitation-delivery/7-language-and-communication).
2. Training gains, generalization, maintenance and everyday communication are **four separate outcomes**. In Big CACTUS, computer-supported practice improved personally relevant trained-word naming, without improving conversation. The iTalkBetter item-randomized trial showed trained-item naming benefits and improvement in a naturalistic speech task; this is promising but not automatically generalizable to a different app. [Big CACTUS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6700375/) · [iTalkBetter 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38685927/).
3. Distinguish semantic retrieval, phonological processing, syntax, discourse, comprehension, executive function, visual-spatial scanning and motor action. These have **different denominators and error taxonomies**; averaging them as one «therapy accuracy» is clinically uninterpretable. [ASHA Aphasia Practice Portal](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/aphasia/) · [Canadian VCI rehabilitation](https://www.strokebestpractices.ca/recommendations/vascular-cognitive-impairment/3-cognitive-rehabilitation).
4. Semantic and phonological anomia therapies can have different trained-item and transfer effects. In a 2025 retrospective comparison, phonological treatment yielded better average trained-item gains while semantic treatment yielded somewhat better generalization. SFA feature-generation quality and PCA components deserve different measurement. [Treatment comparison 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40009493/) · [PCA systematic review 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40015347/) · [SFA dose/features](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29497754/).
5. Human-reviewed discourse outcomes may measure main concepts, correct information units, story grammar, coherence and transaction success; automated estimates are not automatically verified outcomes. [AphasiaBank discourse](https://talkbank.org/aphasia/discourse/) · [discourse assessment methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8915263/).
6. The reliability of AI transcriptions on aphasic speech is not sufficient to infer every phonological error, particularly without clinician adjudication. Store provenance, uncertainty and abstentions. [SONIVA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13623922/).
7. For visual neglect and arm recovery, gaming can support **practice** but a touchscreen response is not a standardized measure of neglect or motor severity. Use relevant clinical external anchors (e.g. Catherine Bergego Scale/KF-NAP for neglect; FMA-UE/ARAT for upper limb). [Canadian visual rehabilitation](https://www.strokebestpractices.ca/recommendations/stroke-rehabilitation-delivery/8-visual-and-visual-perceptual-impairment) · [CAULIN arm measures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8573909/).

### Evidence-label definitions
- **D (direct mechanism evidence)**: a recognizable therapeutic approach/construct has peer-reviewed clinical evidence (not necessarily this digital game).
- **T (translated construct)**: supported assessment, impairment or rehab target; game-specific adaptation and eight stages are unvalidated.
- **E (exploratory)**: plausible useful rehearsal/engagement, but weak direct evidence for this precise game or digital progression ladder.
These are **qualitative literature judgements**, not formal evidence grades; the 29 game versions themselves have **no assumed clinical validation**.

## One common contract, 29 distinct constructs

| Game (slug) | One primary difficulty axis; optional secondary | Specific event telemetry and targeted probe | What must NEVER be claimed | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Name That Photo** (photo-naming) | Lexical retrieval difficulty (word frequency/imageability); secondary semantic competitor density | Independent naming first-attempt, trained/untrained target, response latency if valid, semantic vs phonemic paraphasia with human verification, phonemic/semantic model cue required, item exposure, delayed naming | ASR lexical-match alone proves independent retrieval; practicing same image proves transfer | D |
| **Word Finding** (word-finding) | Functional/contextual retrieval with intended meanings and acceptable alternatives; secondary topic novelty | Communicated target concept in changing context, acceptable synonyms, circumlocution/self-cue, targeted independent word attempt, support modality, intent interpretation confidence | Identical to isolated picture naming; exact-keyword failure means no communication | T |
| **Two Clues** (two-clues) | Semantic cue diagnosticity/convergence; secondary lexical frequency | Target/acceptable alternatives, clue diagnosticity, confusion with competing candidates, first independent answer, extra delivered cue and timing, response class | Increasing the number of hint words or fading cues alone equals progression | T |
| **Semantic Feature Analysis** (semantic-features) | Target imageability/abstractness; keep required feature count stable | Patient-generated **valid, nonredundant, discriminative** features by type, independence, chart/model prompt, spontaneous target naming before/after features, generalization probe | Number of words or repeated supplied features equals semantic recovery | D |
| **Synonym Generator** (synonym-generator) | Sense specificity/semantic competition; secondary word frequency within tier | Meaning preserved in **sentence context**, valid paraphrase/near-synonym alternatives, inappropriate-sense substitutions, semantic cue, independent versus recognition response | Only one dictionary word is acceptable; high word count means lexical precision | T |
| **Meaning Match** (meaning-match) | Semantic distance between target and foil; secondary word vs sentence unit | Tap/selection accuracy, foil class (semantic vs unrelated), reaction after playback, replays, ambiguity, cue visibility, first independent *receptive* choice | Tap correctness is expressive naming, articulation or speech accuracy | T |
| **Category Fluency** (category-fluency) | Semantic search and switching demand; secondary standardized window | Verified unique valid items, semantic clusters and switches, perseverations, intrusions, within-interval output, prompt type; separate articulation/ASR gaps | Raw word count alone means better cognition or language | T |
| **Describe & Guess** (describe-guess) | Target opacity/imageability; no second axis preferred | Informative/diagnostic features, target-name avoidance (if trained), multimodal strategy used, trained vs novel listener, communicative success **confirmed by partner**, actual cue help | An AI guess alone establishes communication success | T |
| **Phrase Practice** (phrase-practice) | Functional script complexity: communicative intent and meaningful phrase units; secondary listener/context novelty | Completeness of requested message, trained phrases, model/unison/phonemic cue history, independently attempted phrase, timing, intelligibility when verified, transfer to changed partner, retention | Modeled repetition is independently spontaneous functional speech | D/T |
| **Minimal Pairs** (minimal-pairs) | Auditory phoneme-contrast discrimination; secondary segment position/acoustic similarity | Heard pair and contrast, correct receptive selection, confusability matrix, speaker/replay count, audio presentation, response time when reliable, channel='tap' | Tap is production phoneme accuracy or absence of apraxia | T |
| **Phonological Awareness** (phonological-awareness) | Sublexical phoneme operation complexity (identify, blend, segment, delete/manipulate); secondary lexicality | Operation type/position/cluster, phonemes targeted, answer type, tap/spoken mode, acoustic replay, error operation, clinician-verified phoneme if spoken | Orthography or typed letter choice alone measures spoken phonology | D/T |
| **Fix the Sentence** (fix-sentence) | Semantic/morphological/syntactic repair complexity, **predeclare target construct per item**; secondary number of errors | Error type, position, detection vs correction, phase 1/2 source, morphological agreement, pre/post repair, accepted alternatives, tap/typed/mixed/voice, cue and model | A tapped or typed repair is independently produced spoken sentence | T |
| **Sentence Construction** (sentence-construction) | Syntactic structure/role reversibility (canonical → noncanonical/embedded); no second axis | Subject–verb–object/thematic roles, tense/agreement, constituent order, clause depth, correct structure, semantic reversibility, assistance, independent production | Word overlap or more words alone shows syntactic mastery | D |
| **Sentence Game** (sentence-game) | Functional message formulation/completion in scenarios; secondary clause/role complexity | Essential intent slots filled, listener-understandable message, syntactic ordering, independent vs modeled output, real source, clinician rubric for multiple valid sentences | Same clinical outcome or state as Sentence Construction | T |
| **Follow Directions** (follow-directions) | Auditory command complexity: step depth and conditional/temporal order; secondary reversibility | Correct actions and sequence, omitted actions, conditional errors, audio replay, visible text support, attention vs hearing confounds, execution modality | Correct taps are expressive speech or prove everyday command-following | T |
| **Narrative Retell** (narrative-retell) | Story grammar/macrostructure (episodes, main concepts, causal structure); secondary delayed retell | Clinician-reviewed essential main concepts, events in order, referents, causal links, CIUs where reliable, trained vs untrained story, preview/replay, support | Length, lexical similarity or AI fluency score alone measures story comprehension | T |
| **Thought Continuation** (thought-continuation) | Contextual under-specification / need to generate a proposition; secondary topic abstractness | Relevant novel propositions, local coherence, topic maintenance, source of prompt, support, contradictions, sustained turns; human-rated on a fixed rubric | More speech, longer pauses or model praise equals propositional progress | E |
| **Thought Organization** (thought-organization) | Propositional ordering and coherence; secondary required relation type (cause/time) | Idea units and logical order, connectors, repetition, missing premise, revisions, explicit scaffold use, output mode | A neat typed list alone proves independent spoken discourse organization | T/E |
| **Abstract Compare** (abstract-compare) | Concept pair abstractness/imageability; secondary overlap between concepts | Correct similarity and **difference** dimensions, discriminative evidence, relation accuracy, conceptual contradictions, cue/model dependence, rubric raters | Education knowledge, length or embedding similarity is automatically clinical recovery | E |
| **Smart Coach** (conversation-coach) | Structured interaction complexity (intent, turns, repair opportunities); secondary situation novelty | Supported scripts/functional requests, turn goals, initiation, actual repair, explicit modeling, human-rated listener success; keep **scripted** vs conditional AI replies distinct | Four scripted turns prove spontaneous dialogue or generalization | D/T |
| **Free Talk** (conversation-partner) | Pragmatic contingency (self-initiation, topic, response to unexpected partner behavior); secondary social demand | Independent initiations, successful clarifications/repairs, communication breakdowns, partner interventions, topic shifts, human-rated functional success | Bot-generated praise or turns count alone proves authentic conversation | T |
| **Detective Mind** (detective-mind) | Inferential load/implicitness and plausible alternatives; secondary relevant-clue memory load | Clue IDs, evidence relevance, fact-vs-inference category, causal/bridging inference, competing hypotheses, replay, explanation rubric, whether answer is receptive vs open | Longer/harder vocabulary or arbitrary puzzle tiers indicates clinical inference improvement | E/T |
| **Multi-Step Planning** (multi-step-plan) | Goal-plan depth/subgoal nesting; secondary constraints | Step selection/order, omissions, violated constraints, plan revisions, use of planning aid, generalization to functional plan verified by clinician/caregiver | Speed or number of taps alone means executive-function recovery | T |
| **Sequence Builder** (sequence-builder) | Temporal/causal event ordering; secondary sequence length only if calibrated | Ordered-step accuracy, inversions, missing events, cause–effect justification, first-step hint, supported vs independent sequence | Equivalent to open-ended planning or real activity completion | T |
| **Pattern Match** (pattern-match) | **Construct choice required**: visual recognition/working-memory span *or* rule induction; secondary distraction only after fixing target | Span, preview/delay, ordered hits, substitution/perseveration, switch costs *if rule induction*, visual/hand confounds | Automatically measures aphasia or that a longer pattern is the same as a rule-switch | E |
| **Dual-Load Naming** (dual-load-naming) | Secondary-task interference while primary lexical targets stay stable; secondary overlap timing | Single-task baseline, dual-task naming accuracy & latency, **interference cost**, secondary-task performance, error type, same target set, whether cue helped | A raw slower latency alone is aphasia deterioration; harder words + harder secondary task isolate attention | T |
| **Left-Side Hunt** (left-side-hunt) | Lateralized visual search demand; secondary distractor density | Target x/y region, left vs right misses, search/tap order, cue use, retries, contrast/screen layout, field/vision issues; **touches are not eye-gaze** | Game misses alone diagnose neglect or show recovery on CBS/KF-NAP | T |
| **Reach & Tap** (reach-tap) | Reaching precision/control at safe target positions; secondary target size | Limb/hand used, target position/size, hit/miss, tap interval, usable movement-path data if measured, device/mounting, rest, compensatory hand, fatigue reports | Touchscreen speed or accuracy alone is FMA-UE or ARAT improvement | T |
| **Command Scenes** (supplemental, functional-scenes) | Functional auditory instruction integration in a scene; secondary temporal condition/order | Command components correct, scene-specific action success, sequence, repeats, assistance, source modality, response reliability, novel-scene probe | App clicks prove real-world independent daily-living function | T |

**Warning on content mismatch:** In the retained *Seventeen Games* research, Pattern Match is framed as **rule induction**, while the current eight-level design registry emphasizes **visual working memory**. These should be explicitly separated or a primary target chosen, because their trial metrics and level criteria differ. Likewise, the latest nonclinical Detective Mind L8 scenarios focus on novel abstract hypotheses; the prior proposed L8 ladder emphasized **implicit social/emotional inference**. These are not automatically equivalent clinical bands. Preserve the higher-level tasks as nonclinical until the construct is reconciled with clinician review.

## Trial data contract: common fields, game-specific measurements

**Every raw attempt, including error, skip, and unsupported attempt, needs:**
- Immutable attempt ID, session ID, patient-profile ID (subject authorization enforced at backend), game slug and version, task/scene/stimulus ID and source/version; whether trained, held-out generalization, or delayed maintenance probe.
- **What was intended:** clinical construct/subconstruct, current persisted clinical level if present, actual selected content tier, one primary difficulty parameter, optional secondary, SLP-approved content/progression version, and task context.
- **What occurred:** source input (\`tap_recognition\`, \`typed\`, \`browser_asr_unverified\`, \`verified_spoken_audio\`, \`clinician_adjudicated\`, \`manual_self_report\`, \`skipped\`, \`mixed\`), attempt count, timing, sensor conditions and meaningful response payload.
- **Exact delivered assistance:** semantic/phonemic/model/choice/visual support, audio replays, on-screen text, whether cue preceded the response, prompted vs independent try. Do **not** call a revealed full target 'phonemic cue'.
- **Scoring provenance:** game-specific item rubric/criterion, accepted alternatives, correctness or *unknown/abstained*, classifier model and version, ASR uncertainty, clinician label and disagreement, failure reason, scoring/rubric version.
- **Multiple separate eligibility flags:** \`receptive_progression_eligible\`, \`expressive_progression_eligible\`, \`speech_accuracy_eligible\`, \`independent_probe_eligible\`, \`functional_transfer_verified\`. Absent/unsupported ≠ false response. The current unified event contract may require a backwards-compatible mapping to these axes; **do not introduce new production field names without schema and migration review**.
- Raw write acknowledgement, final session commit/read-back, ownership/RLS evidence, audit timestamps. Use minimum necessary data collection, explicit recording consent and retention policy.

**Three distinct numerical/interpretive layers:**
1. **Training / adaptive feedback:** can use supported successes, assistance needed, effort and item-specific improvement to adjust practice. Label as training telemetry.
2. **Clinical progression:** only game-specific *eligible* conditions at a defined level/construct, in an approved rubric and adequate data window. No cross-game universal 80% threshold has direct supporting trial evidence. A correct tapped receptive choice can support an approved receptive ladder but **never** automatically become independent speech production.
3. **Outcome / generalization:** fixed-form or item-balanced held-out baseline and follow-up probes, clinician-adjudicated speech/discourse if required, maintenance probes after delay, plus standardized functional measures outside the game. Keep transfer separate from training.

### Research-to-app reporting: clinician-facing view
For every game report: **target construct**, tier definition, sample size/denominator, independent valid successes, assisted successes by support type, omissions/errors, unverified abstentions, dose/exposures, trained vs untrained probes, repeat reliability, and whether any clinician adjudicated the sample. If the evidence is insufficient, show **insufficient verified data** rather than 0% or an inferred promotion.

**Do not compute a single "overall NeuroSpark therapy accuracy" from naming, receptive choices, discourse, reasoning, motor taps and spatial search.** Their score units and denominators differ. The existing project *Admin Analytics for Stroke Therapy Outcomes by Clinical Profile* proposed an average session accuracy; that is only useful when narrowed to comparable constructs/protocols and adjusted for support, difficulty, measurement version and patient mix. Preserve a descriptive *dose/adherence* overview separately.

### External outcomes (not silently reimplemented as game scores)
- Aphasia impairment: an SLP-chosen standardized battery (e.g. CAT or WAB-R as appropriate), standardized naming and comprehension subtests; account for culturally and linguistically appropriate versions, training/proprietary terms and licensing.
- Connected speech: clinician-adjudicated AphasiaBank-style elicitation with main concepts, correct information units, story grammar, coherence under repeatable prompts; rater training and interrater assessment required.
- Functional communication and participation: consider ACOM, CETI, CADL-3 or appropriate measures with the actual licensed administration and its published interpretation rules. Do not assert in-game chat is one of these tests. [Aphasia functional outcome review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12065072/).
- Executive/attention/memory: standardized neuropsychological assessment in context of aphasia, sensory deficits and disability; compare to functional planning goals rather than puzzle score.
- Spatial neglect: clinician-administered CBS via KF-NAP or other valid measures; account for vision and body neglect. [CBS/KF-NAP](https://www.sralab.org/rehabilitation-measures/catherine-bergego-scale).
- Upper limb: therapist-administered FMA-UE, ARAT or other appropriate motor measures; digitizer tapping alone is not these outcomes. [CAULIN](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8573909/).

## Clinician calibration and validation, in order
1. **Choose the construct** for each game. Resolve any conflict between research ladder, current item-bank properties and actual mechanic. Label any exploratory game appropriately.
2. **Write a level blueprint** defining invariant skill, one primary axis, optional secondary, content examples and excluded confounds for *all 8 proposed levels*. Keep support separate and never unlock unimplemented content.
3. **Define a scoring/rater rubric** with accepted alternatives, errors, partial-credit rules and distinct input modes. Adjudicate borderline aphasic outputs with at least two clinicians for a calibration set. Calculate interrater agreement.
4. **Establish assessment probes** with held-out, less-rehearsed stimuli; distinguish baseline, acquisition, generalization, maintenance, and participation outcomes. Blind rating where feasible.
5. **Validate ASR and NLP per task, not globally** with consented representative aphasic speech and silent/noisy conditions. Record WER/CER plus **clinically important error concordance** (semantic vs phonological vs omissions, phrase meaning, pauses), calibration and abstention rate. No invented audio correctness for typed/manual trials.
6. **Build software contract tests** proving level selector chooses correct content, no L8 basic-item fallback, no duplicate support/score, exact event fields, patient isolation, raw persistence/read-back and UI microphone safety. Use dedicated test backend; real authenticated/RLS verification remains required.
7. **Clinician pilot** for usability, fatigue, support accessibility, task validity, and functional transfer. Evaluate dose and adverse frustration burden; ensure the person can use a single spoken Start flow.
8. Only then **consider** activation of game-specific longitudinal progression thresholds, with approved version and clinician override. Eight proposed levels are internal practice tiers until clinical evaluation establishes validity; no published paper proves the exact 8-step NeuroSpark ladders.
